Bottom line vs. your $93,637 true total W2 value; target bonus not included
What would actually match your current package: roughly $68–72/hr full-time C2C, once self-employment tax, lost benefits, and contractor overhead are priced in. That still excludes your $14,283 target bonus (you've already banked $17.6K in incentive pay and stock awards YTD, which C2C has no equivalent of). An S-corp election on the C2C side, paying yourself a "reasonable salary" and taking the rest as distributions, could claw back roughly $3,500–4,500/yr of the gap by shrinking the self-employment tax base, at the cost of running payroll and ~$800–1,500/yr in extra accounting.

Assumptions worth checking

  • Full-time, all 2,080 hours billed. Real contract work rarely hits 100%; every unpaid week is −$2,000 off the C2C gross.
  • Straight 1099, no entity. Most "C2C" postings assume you already have an LLC; ask whether this one does.
  • Individual health plan estimated at ~$500/mo for a single PPO in Virginia. Get an actual marketplace quote before deciding.
  • No employer match assumed on the C2C side. A Solo 401(k) lets you self-fund up to $70K/yr, but every dollar comes out of your own net, not free money.
  • 2025 tax brackets used as a stand-in for 2026. Swap in real numbers once 2026 figures are published.

Georgia runs the opposite direction from NYC. Atlanta's flat 5.39% state tax (vs. Virginia's 5.75% top rate and NY's State+city stack) and lower typical rent mean your current dollar stretches further, not less — a GA offer at or even slightly below your $119K base can hold your Lorton lifestyle, where the same move to Brooklyn needs a raise just to break even. Use the Virginia / New York City / Georgia selector in the W2 vs C2C tab to price a specific offer; Atlanta market salary ranges (the equivalent of the table above) still need a fresh aggregator pull before I put hard numbers here.

How the parity number was built

  • Held your non-housing budget (401k, insurance, groceries, everything else) constant in dollar terms, then solved for the NYC gross salary whose after-tax income covers $2,800 rent and leaves the same monthly surplus your $2,096.54 mortgage leaves today.
  • NY State + NYC resident tax stack on top of federal and FICA. Virginia has no city-level income tax, so this is the single biggest driver of the gap beyond rent itself.
  • Rent vs. mortgage isn't fully apples-to-apples: your mortgage payment builds home equity; rent doesn't. The targets above match cash flow, not net worth.
  • Market figures are 2026 aggregator averages (Indeed, ZipRecruiter, Glassdoor) for "Security Engineer" titles broadly. Actual offers hinge on company tier, so sanity-check against Levels.fyi for specific employers.
